The Project

A Beginning Farmer and 4-H Youth Agriculture Complex on the County's Clark Farm property. The 65-acre main parcel already has a farmhouse (the Red House), barns, cattle pens, and pasture. The master plan puts them back to work as a teaching farm, with room to add barns, an arena, and an event space as programs grow.

The plan serves two cattle programs (a Beginning Rancher herd on pasture and 4-H cow and calf projects in pens), swine, sheep, goats, and poultry, horticulture, and the people who teach, learn, and visit.

The land sets the herd, the programs set the buildings, and the Levels let it grow.

Design Drivers

  • Pasture is scarce

    The herd is sized to the pasture the site has, the resource this community is short on.

  • Reuse first

    The Red House and existing barns go back to work first, keeping the Old Florida character and limiting costs.

  • Grow by module

    Pens and barns are added in modules as programs fill up, not locked to a fixed animal count.

  • Protect the water

    Water quality is built into the design and daily operations: grazing, washdown, and manure management.

The Program

Animals

  • Beginning Rancher herd: lives on pasture, with fencing, water, and a corral with chute and scale.
  • 4-H cow and calf projects: one project per 12' × 24' pen, with turnout, washdown, and feed and tack storage.
  • Swine, sheep, and goats: shaded, well-ventilated 6' × 10' pens; any pen can take any of the three.
  • Poultry: predator-safe housing, apart from the other animals.

People

  • Teaching: a classroom and workshop for about 50, and a demonstration kitchen.
  • Meeting: a conference room for 20 to 25, near the lobby and restrooms.
  • Staff: offices for 5 staff, with sight of the entry.
  • Growing and showing: a horticulture teaching area, and space for demonstrations, turnout, and community events.
